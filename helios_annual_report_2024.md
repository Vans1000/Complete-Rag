HELIOS ENERGY GROUP
Consolidated Annual Operations Report — Fiscal Year 2024
Prepared for the Board of Directors | 14 February 2025


================================================================
SECTION 1 — EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW
================================================================

Helios Energy Group (HEG) is a diversified renewable energy company
headquartered in Reykjavik, Iceland, with regional operations across
four continents. Founded in 2008 by Dr. Astrid Voss and Kenji Nakamura,
the company operates five business divisions: Solar, Wind, Hydro,
Battery Storage, and Advanced Research.

In fiscal year 2024, Helios Energy Group reported consolidated revenue
of €4.72 billion, an 18.4% increase over the prior year's €3.99 billion.
Net income reached €612 million, up from €487 million in 2023. Total
installed generating capacity grew from 14.2 GW to 18.7 GW. The
company now employs 11,340 people worldwide.

CEO Dr. Astrid Voss described 2024 as "the year Helios crossed the
threshold from regional operator to global player." The company's
flagship achievement was the completion of the Aurora Offshore Array
in the North Sea, the largest single wind installation in company
history.

Chief Financial Officer Marcus Ólafsson noted that gross margin
improved to 41.3%, driven primarily by falling turbine costs and
improved battery cell pricing. The company ended the year with
€1.94 billion in cash and equivalents and a net debt position of
€2.31 billion.

Helios operates under a "Three Horizon" strategy: Horizon One
optimizes existing assets; Horizon Two scales proven technologies in
new markets; Horizon Three incubates experimental technologies. In
2024, the company allocated 62% of capital expenditure to Horizon One,
27% to Horizon Two, and 11% to Horizon Three.

SECTION 2 — SOLAR DIVISION
================================================================

The Solar Division operates utility-scale photovoltaic installations
across southern Europe, North Africa, and Australia. Division revenue
in 2024 was €1.41 billion, representing 29.9% of group revenue.

Total installed solar capacity reached 7.2 GW at year-end, up from
5.9 GW. The division's largest single asset is the Ouarzazate
Expansion Project in Morocco, a 1.8 GW installation that came online
in March 2024. Ouarzazate alone supplies enough electricity to power
approximately 1.4 million Moroccan households.

Solar Division head Priya Ramaswamy oversees 3,100 employees. She
joined Helios in 2019 from Tata Power Renewables. Under her leadership,
the division has reduced per-watt installed cost from €0.62 in 2019 to
€0.31 in 2024 — a 50% reduction.

The division operates three manufacturing partnerships: with LONGi
in Malaysia, with First Solar in Ohio, and with Meyer Burger in
Germany. Combined, these partners supplied 4.1 GW of modules in 2024.

Notable solar projects completed in 2024:
  - Ouarzazate Expansion (Morocco) — 1.8 GW, online March 2024
  - Almería Agrivoltaic (Spain) — 340 MW, online July 2024
  - Pilbara Solar Farm (Australia) — 620 MW, online November 2024
  - Benban Phase III (Egypt) — 480 MW, online September 2024

The Almería project is notable as Helios' first large-scale
agrivoltaic installation, combining solar generation with olive grove
cultivation across 1,200 hectares. Preliminary data suggests crop
yields improved 12% under partial shading.

Solar Division faces headwinds from grid interconnection delays in
Spain and Italy, where queue times now average 42 months. The division
is lobbying for regulatory reform through the European Solar Council.

SECTION 3 — WIND DIVISION
================================================================

The Wind Division operates both onshore and offshore installations,
primarily in the North Sea, Baltic Sea, and along the Atlantic coast
of Portugal. Division revenue in 2024 was €1.68 billion, representing
35.6% of group revenue — the largest division by revenue.

Total installed wind capacity reached 8.1 GW, up from 6.4 GW. This
includes 3.2 GW offshore and 4.9 GW onshore.

The Aurora Offshore Array, completed in August 2024, added 2.4 GW of
offshore capacity across 187 turbines located 62 km off the Yorkshire
coast. The array cost €7.8 billion to construct and represents the
single largest capital project in Helios history. Each turbine is a
15 MW Vestas V236-15.0 unit.

Wind Division head is Henrik Lindqvist, a 20-year veteran of Ørsted
who joined Helios in 2021. He oversees 4,200 employees. The division
maintains its own marine operations base in Hull, England, with
four specialized installation vessels.

Onshore wind capacity is concentrated in Portugal (1.9 GW), Poland
(1.4 GW), and Romania (1.6 GW). The Romanian portfolio includes the
Dobrogea cluster, which has achieved a 97.2% availability rate — the
highest in the company's portfolio.

Offshore wind faces margin pressure from rising steel prices and
specialized vessel scarcity. Turbine installation costs rose 14% in
2024 compared to 2023. Henrik Lindqvist has publicly advocated for
long-term offtake agreements to stabilize revenue.

A notable setback: the Baltic Sea Kaskasi Extension was delayed by
11 months due to environmental review, pushing first power from
September 2024 to August 2025. The delay cost approximately
€140 million in carrying costs.

SECTION 4 — HYDRO DIVISION
================================================================

The Hydro Division operates pumped-storage and run-of-river
facilities, primarily in Norway, Iceland, and the Swiss Alps. Division
revenue in 2024 was €684 million, or 14.5% of group revenue.

Total hydro capacity stands at 2.6 GW, essentially unchanged from
2023 due to the division's strategic focus on storage optimization
rather than new build.

The division operates 23 facilities, of which 14 are pumped-storage
and 9 are run-of-river. The largest is the Kárahnjúkar Optimization
Project in eastern Iceland, a 690 MW pumped-storage facility that was
retrofitted in 2024 with new variable-speed pump-turbines from Andritz.

Hydro Division head Elena Brunner leads 1,340 employees. She is a
third-generation hydro engineer whose grandfather worked on the
original Swiss dam projects of the 1950s.

The division's core strategic value is grid stabilization. Hydro
assets provided 41% of Helios' total grid-balancing services in 2024,
despite representing only 14% of capacity — a reflection of pumped
storage's unique ability to respond to frequency deviations within
milliseconds.

In December 2024, Helios signed a 15-year grid-stabilization contract
with the Norwegian transmission operator Statnett, worth an estimated
€210 million annually. This contract converts what was previously a
merchant revenue stream into a contracted one.

Environmental groups have raised concerns about the Kárahnjúkar
facility's impact on wild reindeer migration. Helios committed
€14 million to habitat restoration in 2025.


================================================================
SECTION 5 — BATTERY STORAGE DIVISION
================================================================

The Battery Storage Division is Helios' fastest-growing unit. Division
revenue in 2024 was €482 million, up 187% from €168 million in 2023.

Total storage capacity reached 800 MWh, up from 310 MWh. The division
operates 17 grid-scale battery installations, primarily co-located
with solar and wind assets.

Battery Storage Division head is Dr. Yuki Tanaka, a materials scientist
who joined Helios in 2022 from Panasonic's energy division. She leads
380 employees, the smallest headcount among the five divisions.

The division's primary technology is lithium iron phosphate (LFP)
chemistry, sourced from CATL in China and Northvolt in Sweden.
Helios has committed to onshoring 40% of cell supply to European
manufacturers by 2027.

The largest battery installation is the Aurora Co-location Project in
the North Sea, a 220 MWh system directly connected to the Aurora
Offshore Array. It is the first offshore battery deployment of this
scale globally.

Battery Storage faces a critical constraint: cell supply. Global LFP
demand now exceeds announced manufacturing capacity by an estimated
23%. Dr. Tanaka has flagged this as the division's "single greatest
existential risk."

SECTION 6 — ADVANCED RESEARCH DIVISION
================================================================

The Advanced Research Division (ARD) is Helios' Horizon Three
incubator. It does not generate revenue; its 2024 operating cost was
€156 million, all funded from group cash flow.

ARD operates three primary programs: Green Hydrogen, Next-Gen
Turbines, and Grid AI. ARD head is Professor Lars Erikkson, formerly
of KTH Royal Institute of Technology.

The Green Hydrogen program is developing solid-oxide electrolysis at
pilot scale, currently 8 MW at a facility in northern Iceland. Target
is commercial-scale deployment by 2030.

The Next-Gen Turbine program is a partnership with DTU Wind Energy to
develop a 22 MW offshore turbine platform. A 1:10 scale prototype
began testing in October 2024.

The Grid AI program uses reinforcement learning to optimize
multi-asset dispatch across the entire Helios portfolio. Early
results suggest a 6.3% improvement in portfolio-level revenue
optimization versus a rule-based baseline.

ARD filed 47 patents in 2024, up from 31 in 2023. The division
collaborates with 12 universities across Europe and publishes its
foundational research openly.

SECTION 8 — SUSTAINABILITY AND ESG
================================================================

Helios Energy Group's 2024 sustainability performance:

  Scope 1 emissions:     42,000 tCO2e (2023: 51,000 tCO2e)
  Scope 2 emissions:     18,000 tCO2e (2023: 22,000 tCO2e)
  Scope 3 emissions:    410,000 tCO2e (2023: 468,000 tCO2e)
  Emissions intensity:   5.2 gCO2e/kWh (2023: 6.8 gCO2e/kWh)

The company has committed to net-zero Scope 1 and 2 emissions by
2030 and net-zero across all scopes by 2040.

Grid contribution: Helios-generated electricity avoided an estimated
14.6 million tonnes of CO2 in 2024, based on displaced marginal
generation in each market. This is equivalent to removing
approximately 3.2 million passenger vehicles from the road for a year.

Biodiversity commitments:
  - €14M to Icelandic reindeer habitat restoration (see Section 4)
  - €8M to North Sea marine mammal monitoring (Aurora)
  - €3M to Moroccan desert ecosystem research (Ouarzazate)
  - €5M to Portuguese cork oak preservation (onshore wind)

The Almería agrivoltaic project (see Section 2) was highlighted in
the EU's 2024 "Agriculture and Energy Synergies" report as a best-
practice case study.

Governance: The Board has 11 members, 5 of whom are women. Two board
committees — Audit and Sustainability — meet quarterly. In 2024, the
company adopted the TNFD disclosure framework ahead of regulatory
requirement.

SECTION 10 — STRATEGIC PRIORITIES FOR 2025
================================================================

Management has identified six strategic priorities for fiscal 2025:

1. Aurora Availability — reach and sustain 96% availability at the
   Aurora Offshore Array by Q3 2025.

2. Baltic Recovery — bring the Kaskasi Extension to first power by
   August 2025 and recover schedule credibility.

3. Battery Supply — secure 2 GWh of LFP cell supply via contracts
   with Northvolt and at least one additional European supplier.

4. Grid AI Scaling — deploy the Grid AI dispatch system across 80%
   of portfolio capacity by year-end.

5. Hydrogen Commercialization — advance solid-oxide electrolysis
   from 8 MW pilot to 40 MW demonstration.

6. Capital Discipline — maintain net debt / EBITDA below 2.0x while
   funding all six priorities from operating cash flow.

CEO Voss closed the report with a note: "The energy transition is
no longer a question of whether, but of how fast and how well.
Helios is positioned to lead — not by chasing volume, but by
executing on the fundamentals of reliability, cost, and trust."
